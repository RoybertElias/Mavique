QuickBooks vs Xero

A practical way to compare accounting software for a small business.

Editorial guide • Updated October 2026

Start with accounting requirements

Before comparing products, identify what you need: income and expense tracking, invoicing, bank reconciliation, reporting, tax preparation support, user access and integrations.

If an accountant or bookkeeper is involved, include their workflow in the evaluation. A system that looks simple to an owner may create extra work for the finance professional.

Compare the daily workflow

Test common tasks such as creating an invoice, categorizing a transaction, reconciling a bank account and finding a report. Real tasks reveal usability differences more clearly than feature checklists.

Check export options and data access before committing. Your accounting records are important business data, so portability deserves attention.

Think beyond the subscription

Include setup time, migration, training, add-ons and professional support in your total-cost calculation. Also verify current pricing and regional tax/accounting capabilities directly with the provider.

Bottom line

Choose the accounting platform that your business and finance workflow can maintain accurately. Confirm current features, prices and local requirements before purchase.

Editorial note: Product features, prices, availability and policies can change. Verify current details with the provider before making a purchase or business decision.
